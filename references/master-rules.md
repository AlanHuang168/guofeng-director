# Guofeng donghua master rules

Load this reference for every create, derive, optimize, diagnose, variants, or direct-image task. These are the controlling shared rules for `逆仙黑暗`, `斗破热血`, and `古风国漫通用`.

Route files may expand mood, effects, palette, combat, scale, or atmosphere. They must not weaken character readability, identity consistency, composition hierarchy, material credibility, or positive/negative separation. Only an explicit user instruction may override a named master rule; preserve every unaffected rule.

## Core identity

- Build an image that reads as cinematic Chinese guofeng donghua: ancient Chinese fantasy design language, expressive animation character rendering, filmic light, credible materials, and controlled atmosphere.
- Use Chinese visual vocabulary directly: layered robes, tied hair, hair crowns, ribbons, flying sleeves, ancient weapons, jade pendants, fire seals, talismans, palace gates, cliff roads, cloud seas, ruined temples, desert cities, mountain sects, moonlit courts, and heavenly calamity.
- Do not define the style through one repetitive formula. Avoid always using the same moon gate, white jade corridor, blue sky, three-quarter back view, red robe, or circular portal.
- Avoid direct artist imitation. Describe visible cinematic traits, material behavior, color, lens, emotion, and composition instead of named artist style.

## Character clarity as a master rule

- When the user mentions a person, couple, hero, heroine, named role, portrait, reference image, emotion, or interaction, make the character readable rather than treating the figure as a decorative scale dot.
- For `人物优先级：高`, faces must be clearly visible: eyes, brows, nose bridge, mouth, cheek contour, and expression should read. Use front-facing or three-quarter front view unless the user locks another angle.
- For `人物优先级：中`, faces and silhouettes must remain readable while the environment keeps strong narrative presence.
- For `人物优先级：低`, the environment may dominate, but identity-critical clothing, posture, role color, and direction still need clarity.
- Do not bury faces in fog, backlight, hair, sleeves, motion blur, glowing effects, masks, cropped shadows, or excessive distance unless explicitly requested.

## Reference-image consistency

- If reference images are supplied, bind each reference to a role before writing the prompt.
- Preserve stable identity anchors from the reference: face shape, eye spacing, brow shape, nose and mouth proportions, hairstyle, hair ornament, costume silhouette, main colors, signature accessory, weapon, and energy type.
- If two references are supplied, do not swap their traits. Keep each person's hair, outfit, face, and prop attached to the correct role.
- Use references for identity and design continuity, not for copying background clutter, accidental lighting, compression artifacts, watermarks, text, or irrelevant objects.
- When reference visibility conflicts with the requested pose, preserve identity anchors while adapting pose and lighting.
- If no reference image is supplied, create fictional adults and keep descriptions stable enough for later reuse.

## Two-person interaction

- Treat two-person scenes as relational compositions, not as two isolated portraits pasted together.
- Specify each role's side of frame, body angle, gaze line, emotional state, and distance.
- For `牵手`, state which hands meet, where the contact sits in the frame, how sleeves overlap, and how fingers wrap naturally. Preserve clear hand anatomy.
- For `对视`, align faces and eye lines; avoid one person looking at camera while the other looks elsewhere unless requested.
- For `护持`, show protective body blocking, hand placement, cloak or sleeve direction, and the protected person's reaction.
- For `对峙`, use opposing body angles, energy direction, weapon line, spacing, and tension.
- For romantic or tragic interaction, keep expression subtle and readable rather than overusing tears, glow, petals, or melodramatic blur.

## Focal hierarchy

Use one dominant hierarchy:

1. **Dominant**: the face pair, single protagonist, combat action, flame core, calamity gate, giant structure, or emotional moment.
2. **Supporting**: background architecture, energy effect, secondary figure, weapon, cloud, moon, cliff, fire storm, or light shaft.
3. **Texture**: sparks, rain, ash, robe embroidery, broken stones, talisman fragments, hair strands, distant silhouettes, small lights, or dust.

- Do not assign equal contrast, size, saturation, sharpness, and detail to every object.
- When character priority is high, background spectacle must frame or pressure the characters without stealing facial focus.
- When environment priority is high, characters may be smaller, but the prompt must still explain their role in the scene.

## Guofeng construction and costume language

- Use ancient Chinese fantasy construction without limiting the world to strict historical reconstruction.
- Favor layered roofs, long stairs, open terraces, cliff temples, stone gates, pavilions, desert fortresses, sect plazas, ritual platforms, hanging banners, giant doors, and mountain paths.
- Keep physically readable structures: believable thickness, load, joints, worn edges, cracks, moisture, soot, carved depth, and material response.
- Costumes should have visible structure: inner robe, outer robe, belt, shoulder or collar shape, sleeve length, hem layers, fabric weight, hair ornament, and controlled accessories.
- Use `衣有骨、纱有气、饰有止、色有主`: cloth has structure, gauze has airflow, ornaments stop before clutter, one color family leads.
- State one coherent wind or motion direction when hair, sleeves, cape, flame, smoke, dust, or ribbons move.

## Cinematic material and rendering

- Skin should remain natural and clear, with controlled highlights and no waxy plastic texture.
- Silk should show directional folds, weight, translucency where appropriate, and wind response.
- Metal should show edge highlights, patina, engraved depth, and contact wear.
- Jade, stone, lacquer, wood, bronze, flame, lightning, cloud, smoke, water, ash, and dust should be materially distinct.
- Energy effects should have a source, direction, color core, edge falloff, and effect on nearby light and fabric.
- Atmosphere should separate planes. Do not use uniform gray fog as a substitute for depth.

## Scale and environment

- Use scale when it supports the route: giant heavenly gates, ruined palaces, cliffs, deserts, flame columns, cloud seas, calamity vortices, or mountain sects.
- Prove giant scale through visible comparisons: adult-to-gate, body-to-column, figure-to-cliff, bridge-to-palace, flame-to-city wall, staircase repetition, off-frame architecture, or distant settlement.
- Character priority may enlarge people without killing scale. Use background, cropping, lens compression, foreground frames, and off-frame masses to keep grandeur.
- Do not default all characters to 1–4% of frame height. That scale is reserved for environment-led or giant-scale pressure scenes.

## Color and light

- Use route-specific color grammar, but always keep skin tone, face planes, and important costume details readable.
- Keep a clear chroma budget: neutral base, controlled secondary color, and one high-impact accent.
- Use one motivated key light plus readable fill. High contrast may be severe, but it must not erase face structure or hand contact.
- Avoid global orange-gold wash, dirty gray grading, crushed blacks, blown-out whites, and neon overload unless a controlled route explicitly needs a small accent.
